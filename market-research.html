SELF-CREATED DEMONSTRATION PROJECT

Market Research Brief: Residential Cleaning Services in Vietnam

Prepared by Chi Tham Le · Desk-research example · November 2026

Important scope note: This is a demonstration of research structure and analytical approach, not a verified market-size study. No unsupported market-share, revenue or demand figures are presented. The observations below are working hypotheses that require primary research and source verification.

1. Research objective

Explore potential customer segments for residential cleaning services and create a practical framework a small cleaning business could use to plan customer acquisition.

2. Initial customer segments

SegmentPotential need to investigatePossible decision factors
Busy working householdsTime-saving recurring home cleaningTrust, schedule flexibility, transparent pricing
Families with young childrenRoutine cleaning and hygiene supportStaff reliability, safety practices, service consistency
Apartment residentsOne-time or recurring apartment cleaningConvenient booking, building access, clear service scope
Landlords and property managersCleaning between tenant move-in and move-outFast turnaround, checklist, invoice and coordination
Small offices and studiosScheduled workplace cleaningReliability, after-hours availability, agreed checklist

3. Competitor review framework

For each competitor, collect only publicly available business information and record the date checked. Compare:

  • Service coverage and geographic area.
  • Published service packages and price transparency.
  • Booking channels and response options.
  • Publicly stated service guarantees or quality controls.
  • Review themes, while avoiding treating individual reviews as representative market statistics.

4. Customer acquisition hypotheses

  • Local search visibility may matter when customers need a provider in a specific district.
  • Clear package descriptions may reduce uncertainty about what is included.
  • Trust signals such as transparent policies, staff procedures and verifiable reviews may influence consideration.
  • Property managers may require a different offer and sales process from individual households.

These are hypotheses for testing, not established findings. Validate through interviews, search-result sampling and competitor checks in the chosen city.

5. Recommended validation plan

  1. Select one city or district and define the service radius.
  2. Review 15–20 local providers using public business pages and official websites.
  3. Record service types, published prices (if any), booking channels and source URLs.
  4. Interview 5–10 potential customers or business buyers with consent.
  5. Compare observed needs with the initial segments and revise the target profile.

6. Deliverables for a client

  • Competitor comparison spreadsheet with source links and checked dates.
  • Customer segment table with needs and decision factors.
  • Short findings summary separating evidence, interpretation and open questions.
  • Recommended next research steps.

7. Research quality and limitations

This brief does not estimate total addressable market or claim statistically representative customer demand. Before making commercial decisions, the client should validate findings in the exact target location and date range. Public listing data can be incomplete or outdated; each record should be rechecked before outreach.
